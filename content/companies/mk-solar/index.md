## Company Overview

MK Solar recovers rare earth metals from industrial waste and end-of-life permanent magnets. The company runs an eco-friendly urban mining process that purifies and regenerates these materials back to near-new quality.

Rare earth magnets are critical inputs for semiconductors, batteries, and motors, but Korea imports almost all of this material. MK Solar's process helps localize supply and build a circular flow for high-value metals.

## Technology & Product

The core product is a rare earth and valuable-metal recovery solution. It extracts high-purity rare earth components from waste magnets and electronic scrap. The process uses eco-friendly leaching and high-purity separation, and keeps the recycled output at a quality and performance level close to new magnet material.

MK Solar also offers a purification solution for waste acid and spent organic solvents. The process cleans waste phosphoric acid, processing oils, and used solvents so they can be reused. This lowers both waste-treatment costs and raw-material purchasing costs for factories, and supports carbon reduction goals.

## Market & Use Cases

The technology fits manufacturers that generate rare earth scrap or chemical waste, including makers of magnets, motors, electronics, and semiconductors. These plants face both waste-disposal costs and high prices for imported rare earth inputs.

Useful pilot settings include factory waste streams where rare earth recovery can replace imported material, and chemical processes where waste acid or solvent recovery can cut disposal volume.

## Traction & References

MK Solar has commercialized rare earth metal recycling and sales, and generates revenue from this business. The company runs a dedicated R&D lab at the Asan Tangjeong Unicorn Knowledge Industry Center to advance its metal separation process.

MK Solar was selected for the 2026 Gangnam-gu Test-Bed program, which supports pilot deployment of its technology.

## Collaboration Relevance

MK Solar can work with magnet and motor manufacturers, electronics and semiconductor plants, and chemical processors that want to recover value from their waste streams. A practical pilot could start with a defined factory waste batch and test recovery purity and yield.

For Korea-Europe collaboration, the company is relevant to critical raw material security, magnet supply localization, and circular-economy programs that aim to reduce dependence on imported rare earth material.
